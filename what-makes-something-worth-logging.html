What Makes Something Worth Logging

Jul 6, 2026 · 5 min read Field Notes

Every entry on this site starts the same way: something crosses our path, and we have to decide whether it's worth writing down. Most things don't make it. That part of the job is invisible from the outside, but it's the only part that actually matters — the log is only as good as what gets left off it.

The Test We Actually Use

We don't have a scoring rubric, and there's no lab behind this — it's a small, opinionated operation with a shared inbox. What we do have is a quieter question: would we still recommend this to a friend six months from now, once the novelty has worn off and the packaging is long gone? A surprising number of well-marketed things fail that test. They photograph beautifully in a launch video and disappoint slowly, one small annoyance at a time, until they're in a drawer. A surprising number of unglamorous things pass it without even trying — they just keep doing the one job they were built for.

The best compliment a product can get from us isn't “impressive.” It's “still in use.”

That's also why the shelves on this site stay fairly narrow — Tech & Gadgets, Home & Living, Outdoor & Active, Gifts & Curiosities. We'd rather log fewer things carefully than try to cover every category at once. A wide, thin site is easy to build and not very useful to read.

What Doesn't Make the Shelf

We're cautious about anything that only works because of a subscription that might not exist in two years, an app that could get discontinued, or packaging that's clearly built for an unboxing video rather than actual daily use. We're also wary of anything that needs a full paragraph of jargon to justify itself — if a product can't explain what it does in one plain sentence, that's usually the product telling on itself.

None of this makes us right every time. Taste is personal, budgets are different, and a shelf that fits one household won't fit the next one over. What we can promise is that nothing gets logged because a brand asked us to, and nothing gets pulled because it stopped paying us to feature it — mostly because, as this site's affiliate disclosure explains, nobody pays us to feature anything in the first place. If a link from here worked out for you, that's the whole point of the exercise. If it didn't, we'd genuinely like to know — the log only gets better when people push back on it.
